CLIENT ROLE PROJECT

CLIENT REPORT

  • How did you decide what content and images to use for your site plan?
    • The way I decided on the content for my site plan was by using a passion of mine that I've had since high school, manga. I've lost count of the times I've had to explain what manga is to others. So, when we were creating site plans, this is the idea that came to me, create a web site that explains the basics of manga to outsiders. My idea was just to explain the way how to read it and the different genres that are written for manga. As for the images I choose, I felt that using Gemini would be the simplest since I don't have good art skills. The images were meant as simple ways of grabbing the user's attention and demonstrating, on one page, the reading method for manga.
  • How did you communicate with your developer? Was there any issues with communication?
    • The way I communicating with my developer was through Microsoft Teams in the Channel for my site plan and in person. If any of them had questions on what I meant by something, they could ask it on the Teams channel or by just walking up to me in class. This allowed me to not have any communication problems.
  • Did your developer meet your expectations for your site?
    • The developers for my site did I nice job with the site, with the expectation of the one for the "Find Style" page. The other pages looked well-made and easy to browse through. However, it seems that the "Find-Style" still needs to be completed, with both creating the style.css file and putting in the links to the other pages.
  • What will you do differently next time you hire a team to do a project for you?
    • Next time, I think I will meet with the whole group together and go over the basics on what I desire for the site, as well as explaining my vision so that it's easier for them to make it a reality.

LEAD DEVELOPER PROJECT

LEAD DEVELOPER REPORT

  • How did you manage your development team and comunicate the tasks for your junior devs?
    • I communicated with my team mostly through Microsoft Teams and the occassional meet up in class. After we got the site plan from the client, I designated the pages for the two junior developers to work on, checking periodically on their progress and giving a hand when needed.
  • How did you communicate with your client? Was there any issues?
    • Funny enough, the client acually sat at my table during class, so any questions I had for him, I could just slide the chair closer and ask him in person. This allowed me to get any updates required from the client almost instantly. Also, if I or my team had anything we needed from the client, the response was fast and effective.
  • How did you handle publishing your clients site and sharing it with the client?
    • I took his site plan and figured out what his vision was, asking for clarification when needed, and designed the html file properly, creating a styles folder in as well to make it more organized for each developer to get their stylesheet correctly. I also put the link to the site on Teams to allow the client access to the finished product at anytime.
  • How was your experience with managing your site with GitHub?
    • To be honest, it was quite interesting. By using GitHub to place the files and update when needed, I was able to keep things well-organized. It also made it much easier for me to both provide the junior developers with the necessary pieces for the site and to get regular updates on their own progress for the site. Even sending out the invites to allow the Junior developers access to the GitHub was simple.

JUNIOR ROLE DEVELOPER PROJECTS

JUNIOR DEVELOPER REPORT

  • How did you communicate with your team lead? Was there any issues?
    • I communicated with them via Teams. That way, if I had any questions on what they needed me to do while I was at home, I could easily post my questions and quickly get an answer. I also located where they sat in class and walked up to them to ask questions then as well.
  • Did you know what was expected of you for each site?
    • I will admit that there was a bit of confusion for me, at first, when it came to which page I was supposed to do for Project A, but after asking the Senior Developer, I got my answer. For Project B, it was much easier and simpler to know which page I was assigned. As for what was expected of me, that was quite easy once I looked over the site plan for each site and asked any little questions. The clients for my sites were quite willing to provide context when needed.
  • Were you able to contribute to your sites?
    • I believe I was 100% able to contribute on both sites. I took my responsibilities seriously when it came to fulfilling my role as a Junior Developer. I also was very polite with the clients when I had a question or favor to ask, especially with Project B when I needed to have the pictures for my page.
  • Did you run into any issues with GitHub and adding your changes to the sites?
    • I can't think of any issues I faced with GitHub or adding my changes for the sites. Once I got the invites for the GitHub sites, I was able to routinely check for updates for the sites quickly. I even opened tabs on my browser that I kept open the whole time to quickly look at the site as a whole and insure that I was properly doing my job.

General Experience and Reflections

  • What was the most challenging part of this project for you? Were you able to overcome it?
    • If I'm being honest, the most challenging part for me was keeping track of all the different sites that I was a part of. Keeping track of all the work that I do for my site, and all the work I've made over the semester, was hard enough. However, once I talked with the teacher about how to access each GitHub site, it became much easier.
  • What did you enjoy most about this project?
    • I think what I found most enjoyable was learning more about the people around me. As a client, it was interesting seeing how others would interpret my vision and seeing the results. As a Lead Developer, it was quite the experience learning how to delegate pieces of the site plan to two others and learning to not be hovering over their shoulders the whole time. As a Junior Developer, it was nice being trusted to work on one piece of the site and having trust in the Lead Developer knowing the right direction we should each be going with the site.
  • What would you do differently if you could do the project again?
    • If I could do it over again, I would work to meet up with each of my groups and get to know each of them better so that I could know how to better mesh with them and each of their thoughts processes.
  • Do you feel more confident in working on real-world projects after this experience? Explain.
    • I honestly do feel more confident in working on real-world projects after this experience. The reason is quite simple. It's like doing anything that seems scary the first, like learning to drive or memorizing a marching band routine. At first, it seems impossible to achieve, but after doing it over and over again, with guidance with more experienced individuals, like parents helping to learn driving, or a band teacher, it becomes much simpler and much less scary.
